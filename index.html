The approach

What I actually believe about training.

Your body adapts. It isn't a machine that breaks.

Technique matters, but it's only part of the picture. How much you're doing, what you're used to, and how fast you've added load all matter too. Being adaptable beats being perfect.

Progress is more than the numbers going up.

Cleaner, more controlled reps are progress too. You don't get stronger because you lift heavier. You lift heavier because you've got stronger.

You don't have to earn the right to train.

You don't need to pass a mobility exam before you're allowed to get strong. We start from where you actually are.

The real question is: what do you want out of it?

Not whether something is theoretically optimal. What outcome do you want, and what's the sensible route there for the life you've actually got.

It was never really about ripped abs.

It's about breaking limits you'd quietly set for yourself and getting a better quality of life out of it.

My story

I didn't come to this from a background in sport.

Back in the day I wanted to work in music. I partied too much, felt depressed and aimless, and didn't have much of a clue what I was doing with myself.

Then I got into martial arts. It was a humbling experience, and it taught me about grit, work ethic and self belief. Those were the first things in my life that felt properly mine.

Encouraged by my big brother, I qualified as a personal trainer to help other people find the same thing. I knew almost nothing back then. After three years I moved to a body transformation studio in London, got seriously lean, and helped a lot of clients do the same.

I wanted to be a better coach than that, because I'd worked out it isn't just about ripped abs. So I invested in myself: courses, mentorships, coaching from some of the best people in the industry. I still have coaches now. I'd rather keep learning than tell you this is how it's always been done.

My goal is to help you figure this fitness stuff out, so that one day you don't need me.

Outside of coaching you'll usually find me doing Olympic lifting or jiu jitsu, badly at first and then slightly less badly. I think that's rather the point.
